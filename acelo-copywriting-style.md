# Acelo Copywriting Style

This skill captures a repeatable copywriting system for Acelo Logistics' website. It was built by studying the copy architecture of Shuttlers.co (a leading African mass-mobility brand) — not to copy their words, but to reverse-engineer the _formulas_ behind high-converting mobility-brand copy — then re-grounding every formula in Acelo's own brand brief (tagline, tone, services, target audiences).

## Acelo brand voice inputs (always apply)

- **Tagline:** Accelerating Africa's Logistics. Seamless. Swift. Sure.
- **Tone pillars:** Professional (expert, organized) · Dynamic & forward-thinking (energetic, solution-oriented) · Reliable & trustworthy (consistent, dependable) · Customer-centric (attentive, responsive) · Confident (poised, authoritative).
- **Register:** Premium and "cinematic," never cutesy. Confident short sentences over long explanatory ones. Read every draft out loud — if it sounds like a startup app trying to be liked, cut it back toward "operations command center" instead.
- **Signature words:** Seamless, Swift, Sure, Accelerate/Accelerated, Precision, Zero-disruption/Zero-stall, Vetted, Professional Pilot (Acelo's term for driver), Scalable, Fleet coordination.
- **Local authority marker:** name real Abuja landmarks (Mabushi, Garki, Apo, Maitama, Kuje, Wuse, Gwagwalada) when relevant — this is Acelo's version of Shuttlers naming Lagos neighborhoods (Lekki, Ajah, Festac) to prove local depth.

## Rule zero: concrete beats vague, every time

Benchmarked against Shuttlers ("Event transportation made easy with us," "Rent a Vehicle for Any Occasion with Shuttlers") and Moniepoint ("Get the card that works," "Bank with Moniepoint"), the pattern is consistent: **every strong hero headline names an actual product/action + a plain payoff.** None of them lead with an abstract brand-statement.

This is a real trap to watch for — "Africa's logistics, accelerated." is exactly the kind of headline to avoid going forward. It sounds like a tagline, not a hero headline: no product named, no concrete action, no specific payoff a reader can picture. Compare:

| Vague (avoid)                                       | Concrete (write this instead)                      |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| "Africa's logistics, accelerated."                  | "Book a Coaster for your next event with Acelo."   |
| "Reliable transport for Nigeria, always in motion." | "School bus hire that shows up, every single day." |
| "Move your business forward."                       | "Rent a Vehicle for Any Occasion with Acelo."      |

If a headline could be pasted onto any transport company's homepage with the name swapped and still make sense, it's too vague — it needs a named product, service, or specific use case in it.

## The universal headline formula

Every strong mobility-brand hero headline studied does one of these — in under 9 words, present tense, no jargon:

**Pattern A — Outcome-first:** `[Get to / Move] [who] [where/how] — [outcome adjective].`

- Acelo example: _"Move your team, on schedule, without the stress."_

**Pattern B — Ease/relief framing:** `[Task] made [easy/simple/seamless] with [brand].`

- Acelo example: _"Event transportation made seamless with Acelo."_

**Pattern C — Command/confidence framing (matches Acelo's premium register best):** `[Bold capability claim]. [Reassurance clause].`

- Acelo example: _"We've got the fleet to match. Precision in motion, every time."_

**Pattern D — Product-first framing (the most concrete, use as the default for Home and every solution page):** `[Get/Rent/Book] a [named product/service] for [specific occasion or use case] with Acelo.`

- Acelo example: _"Rent a Coaster for your next church event with Acelo."_
- Acelo example: _"Get a school bus contract that shows up, every day."_

Default to Pattern D unless there's a specific reason to go more abstract (e.g. a brand-level About page, where "Africa's logistics, accelerated." is fine as a supporting line, just not as the hero headline of a product-facing page).

Always pair the headline with a one-line **subheadline** that adds a concrete proof point or number: _"Trusted by 50+ organizations moving people across Abuja daily."_ Numbers > adjectives. A number with a "+" reads as evidence; an adjective ("amazing," "great") reads as filler — avoid stacking adjectives.

## Inline stat highlighting (body copy device)

Not every proof point needs its own stat card. A pattern worth borrowing from Moniepoint: drop a specific number directly into a body sentence as a highlighted phrase (bold or brand-colored), so the sentence reads naturally but the eye still catches the proof — e.g. _"Enjoy a 99.9% on-time pickup rate and never worry about a delayed driver again."_ Use this inside value-prop paragraphs and FAQ answers where pulling the number into a separate stat block would be overkill.

## Category eyebrow pill

Small pill-shaped label directly above a feature/section headline, naming the category in 1-3 words (e.g. "Savings," "Payment," "Personal Account" on a banking site; Acelo equivalents: "Fleet Partnership," "Rentals," "Event Logistics"). Pairs with the `acelo-website-designer` skill's badge/pill component. Use above H2/H3 feature headlines in value-prop and solutions-grid sections to help a skimming reader locate the section that matters to them.

## Section-heading formula library

Reusable heading shapes, generalized from patterns across mobility/marketplace sites — write Acelo's own wording each time, don't reuse another brand's exact phrase:

| Section purpose       | Heading shape                                     | Acelo-ready example                               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Services overview     | "[Category] we provide across [scope]"            | "Logistics solutions for every kind of movement"  |
| Reason-to-believe     | "Why choose [Brand]"                              | "Why organizations choose Acelo"                  |
| Process explainer     | "How [Brand/Product] works"                       | "How Acelo Fleet Partnership works"               |
| Proof/stats bar       | "[Brand] by the numbers" or "[Brand] performance" | "Acelo in numbers"                                |
| Testimonial intro     | A rhetorical proof-invitation, not a boast        | "Hear it from the people who move with us"        |
| Safety/trust block    | "[Value] is our [priority framing]"               | "Every trip, vetted for your peace of mind"       |
| Partner/earn pitch    | "Why become a [Partner Role] with [Brand]"        | "Why become a Fleet Partner with Acelo"           |
| FAQ intro             | "Everything you need to know about [Product]"     | "Everything you need to know about Acelo Rentals" |
| Final conversion push | Direct, low-friction ask                          | "Ready to move? Talk to Acelo HQ"                 |

## CTA button copy rules

1. **Start with a verb.** "Get Started," "Request a Demo," "Rent a Vehicle," "Become a Partner," "Talk to Acelo HQ."
2. **Be specific to the action**, not generic ("Submit," "Click here," "Learn more" are last resorts). Match the button label to what actually happens next.
3. **Primary CTA per page = one action.** Every landing page should have exactly one primary CTA repeated (hero, mid-page, footer) — secondary actions (view fleet, download app, WhatsApp) stay visually secondary.
4. **Channel-aware CTAs:** Acelo's real booking flow runs through WhatsApp/phone, not self-serve checkout — so primary CTAs should say things like "Message Acelo HQ on WhatsApp" or "Get a Free Quote," not "Book Now" unless a real booking form exists.

## Feature/benefit blurb formula

Each benefit card = **Bold 2-4 word label** + **1-2 sentence plain-English payoff**, written to the reader ("you"/"your"), never internally-focused ("we built X"). Lead with what changes for the customer, then (optionally) how Acelo delivers it.

Template: `[Benefit Label]\n[What the customer gets]. [How Acelo ensures it].`
Example: _"Zero-Disruption Scheduling\nYour event runs on time, even at 500+ attendees. Our dispatch team manages hub rerouting in real time so delays never reach you."_

## Audience-tone modulation (per landing page)

Keep the core Acelo voice pillars constant; shift vocabulary and proof points per audience:

- **Fleet/Vehicle Partner:** Speak owner-to-owner about income and asset protection. Emphasize "vetted network," "assured revenue," "fixed cost per trip," predictable payouts. Avoid hype ("get rich") — Acelo's register is confident, not salesy.
- **Rental (individuals/executives):** Emphasize convenience, punctuality, vehicle quality, professional pilots. Short, clean sentences.
- **Church/Event landing page:** Emphasize scale (multi-unit coordination), reliability under pressure, and stewardship of large groups. Reference conference/convention logistics as a category, not by naming specific past clients unless Acelo approves.
- **School landing page:** Emphasize safety protocols, consistency (term contracts), vetted drivers, parent peace-of-mind. Slightly warmer tone than corporate pages, still professional.
- **Travel & Tour company landing page:** Speak partner-to-partner (B2B2C) — emphasize white-label reliability, interstate/outstation capability, coordination across multiple cities.
- **Hotels landing page:** Emphasize guest experience continuity — airport transfers, executive comfort, on-brand punctuality that reflects well on the hotel.
- **Real Estate landing page:** Emphasize recurring site-inspection logistics, estate-launch event transport, dependable multi-stop routing.
- **Group travel landing page:** Emphasize large-group coordination, cost efficiency per seat, single point of contact for complex itineraries.

## FAQ tone

Formal-yet-accessible. Questions are phrased the way a real customer would ask them ("How much does a full-day rental cost?"), not as marketing headlines. Answers are direct in the first sentence, then add one clarifying sentence max. Group FAQs by audience (Daily/Rental/Business/Fleet Partner/Event) — don't blend all products into one undifferentiated list.

## Words to avoid

Avoid generic SaaS/startup filler that doesn't match Acelo's premium logistics register: "revolutionize," "game-changer," "disrupt," "hustle," excessive exclamation points, emoji in body copy. Avoid vague adjectives without proof ("amazing," "world-class") — replace with a number, a name, or a specific capability.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Headline under 9 words, states outcome or capability
- [ ] Headline names an actual product/service or specific use case — not just an abstract brand statement
- [ ] Subheadline adds one concrete number or proof point
- [ ] Exactly one primary CTA verb-first and repeated consistently
- [ ] Local landmark or Nigeria/Africa framing included where relevant
- [ ] No unproven superlatives; adjectives backed by a number or fact
- [ ] Tone matches the specific audience section above
- [ ] Read aloud — does it sound "Seamless. Swift. Sure." or does it sound generic?
